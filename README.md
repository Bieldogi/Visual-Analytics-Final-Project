Global Life Expectancy Analysis Authors: Marc de los Aires, Biel Domingo 

📌 Project OverviewIn recent decades, the world has undergone an unprecedented demographic and epidemiological transition. As nations develop, a double burden emerges: greater control over health, but new risks like rising obesity.This project analyzes the global health situation by breaking down life expectancy into various parameters, such as infant mortality and national wealth. The primary goal is to identify the best predictors of longevity (e.g., health expenditure, fertility, economy) and classify health profiles to support public policy decision-making.

📊 Data SourcesThe study uses three unified databases from official international sources, covering the period from 1985 to 2016:Health Nutrition and Population Statistics (World Bank): Provides data on life expectancy, child mortality (<5 years), fertility rate, health expenditure (% of GDP and per capita), and Gross National Income (GNI).Obesity among adults (OMS): Contains data on the prevalence of adult obesity standardized by age and sex.World Population: Includes global population data over the last 50 years.

🛠️ Methodology & Tech StackPython & Scikit-Learn (Machine Learning): Regression models, including Random Forest and a Linear Regression baseline, were trained to predict life expectancy based on socio-economic, demographic, and health features.Tableau (Interactive Visualization): Dynamic, animated maps and predictor-vs-target plots were created to explore how global health trends evolve over time.Streamlit (Web Application): A web app was developed to democratize access to the model, allowing users to interactively set parameters (like health expenditure or fertility rate) and get real-time life expectancy predictions.

💡 Key FindingsThe Development Trap (Obesity Paradox): There is a positive association between life expectancy and adult obesity prevalence. However, obesity plays a secondary role; factors like under-5 mortality, income, and health expenditure explain a much larger share of the variance in life expectancy.Efficiency Beyond Wealth: While wealth is a critical factor, its relationship with life expectancy resembles a logarithmic function rather than a linear one. Some countries manage to achieve very high life expectancy despite having limited resources.Fertility and Under-5 Mortality: The reduction of child mortality and fertility rates are the strongest predictors linked to an increase in life expectancy.

🚀 Future Work & LimitationsFuture Analysis: It would be highly valuable to analyze economically developed and undeveloped countries separately to see how feature importance shifts.Limitations: The study was partially limited by the lack of historical data and the accuracy of older metrics.
